The Settle

One hero animation that performs the mission statement instead of sitting next to it: scattered scraps of camp operations fly in, then settle into four labelled lanes feeding a single board.

Why this instead of a hub diagram

Your instinct was modules feeding one platform, and that is right. The trap is the obvious execution: four boxes with arrows pointing at a logo in the middle. Every B2B site has that diagram, it is static, and critically it only shows the after. Your mission statement leads with the before, the chaos and the wasted money, and that is the half a camp director actually recognises.

So the hero shows both, in order. It opens as a mess of the real artifacts operations runs on today, then resolves. The resolved state is the module diagram you wanted, just arrived at rather than asserted.

The one-line version

A hub diagram says "we integrate four modules". The Settle says "we take the mess you are living in and make it one board." Those are different products to a buyer, and only one of them is your mission statement.

What the fragments should be

Real artifacts, in your camp's own vocabulary, never lorem and never generic "Task 1" cards. The recognition is the whole point, a director should see their own week:

  • A text message: "heater out in bathhouse 2, girls sent to 1"
  • A sticky note: "Cabin 12 window — order glass?"
  • A spreadsheet row: Cl 2.4 · pH 7.3 · 4:37pm
  • A whiteboard line: "Tufts arrives Fri, 50pax, COI??"
  • A voicemail slip, a walkie call, a clipboard, a photo of a broken thing

Roughly twelve to sixteen. Fewer reads as decoration, more reads as noise.

Restraint that keeps it from feeling like a toy

  • It settles once, over about 1.2 seconds, then holds. No looping, no scroll-jacking, no parallax. A hero that keeps moving is a hero nobody reads past.
  • Interaction is hover, not choreography. Hovering a lane lifts its fragments and dims the rest, so the four modules become legible on demand.
  • A quiet Replay in the corner, because the first two seconds are the pitch and some people arrive mid-animation.
  • `prefers-reduced-motion` renders the settled state directly. Non-negotiable, and it also means the static screenshot of your hero is the good frame.

2 · The mission, given room

Directly under the hero, on paper, no card, no icon, just large Bitter at a comfortable measure. It reads as a statement of belief rather than a feature blurb:

Our mission is to streamline the chaos of camp operations and eliminate the stress and wasted money that come from scattered systems. We do it by providing systematic workflows and resources that keep your entire operations team on the same page across facilities, pool and waterfront, commissary, and retreats.

Three typos fixed

Your version had recources → resources, opertaions → operations, and a missing comma after facilities. Otherwise it is verbatim. Flagging because you said "as it should appear", and I did not want to silently rewrite your words, or silently ship the typos.

3 · What scattered costs you

5 · A day at camp

The section I would add that you do not have. Follow one real thread all the way through: a counsellor photographs a cracked window at 7:02am → it lands in Facilities → the director assigns it → the part is ordered through Commissary → it closes before the retreat group arrives Friday. Horizontal, timestamped, scroll-revealed.

This is what "same page across modules" means in practice, and it is impossible to say convincingly in a feature grid.

Build notes

  • CSS transforms only. Every fragment is a positioned div moving between two transform states. No canvas, no animation library, no measurable weight.
  • Palette is already yours: pine #1D3A2E ground, paper fragments, sage lanes, ember for the one accent. Bitter for display, Karla for text.
  • Mobile: fewer fragments, shorter travel, same settle. It must not become a wall of overlapping cards on a phone.
  • The hero must not block paint. Text and buttons render immediately; the animation is decoration layered behind, so a slow connection still gets a working page.
Proposal only, nothing built yet. If the direction lands, I would build the hero first as a standalone piece so you can judge the real thing before the rest of the page changes.
